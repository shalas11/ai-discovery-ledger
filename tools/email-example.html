The lead · Mathematics

Mathematician says he and Claude have proved the Riemann hypothesis

Bernhard Riemann in 1863. Image: Wikimedia Commons, public domain.

The Riemann hypothesis is an 1859 conjecture about how prime numbers are spread out. On November 20, mathematician Levent Alpöge posted a four-page note saying he has found a proof, working with Claude.

He says the method also settles the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, a broader form of the same question.

What he released is unusual. There is no written paper. Alpöge posted only a formalization in Lean, a program that checks proofs, split into 14 archive files. He says a written version would run past 250,000 pages.

He credits Claude with key steps near the end. One was the idea of chaining theta lifts about the square root of X times.

Still unverified

No written paper exists, and no outside mathematician has checked the mathematics itself. Alpöge says he does not yet understand much of the argument. MIT's Andrew Sutherland has compiled the Lean code on his own. Kevin Buzzard says one open question is whether the Lean statement really is the Riemann hypothesis.

Also this week

Physics

Claude agents settle two open problems in black hole physics

Anthropic says Claude agents finished a calculation set up in 2003 but never completed. It shows that a transition in a simplified model universe is abrupt, as Hawking and Page predicted for black holes. Both preprints are unreviewed, and Anthropic is the main source.

Computer science

AI system proves a 2008 conjecture on the limits of quantum speedups

Physical Superintelligence says its research system, running on a non-public Anthropic model, proved the Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture. The proof is on arXiv, and four computer scientists have posted a reworked exposition. It is not peer reviewed.

Mathematics

Gemini agents help prove the Courtade–Kumar conjecture

The 2013 conjecture says no one-bit summary of a noisy signal carries more information than reading off a single input bit. Google says agents on Gemini 4 models extended the proof without human input. Google and its co-authors are the only source so far.

Physics

GPT-6 Astra credited with the proof behind a lattice Standard Model

Physicist Nathaniel Craig says he had a strategy but could not prove it in every case, and that GPT-6 Astra led the detailed proof. The model's role rests on Craig's own account, and no outside assessment has been published.

Medicine

Deep learning finds an ECG signal tied to sudden cardiac death

A model trained on more than 440,000 Swedish heart tracings found a high-risk pattern in ordinary ECGs. Nobody yet knows what the signal is biologically, and it is not in clinical use.
